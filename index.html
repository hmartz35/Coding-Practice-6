Methods notebook

Each panel above pulls from a public API. Here is what each source actually is, what it can tell you, and what it can't. Reading a dashboard without knowing its sources is how analysts go wrong.

EIA · oil prices

U.S. GOV · DAILY

The U.S. Energy Information Administration publishes spot prices for Brent (Europe FOB) and WTI (Cushing, OK FOB) daily. It is the canonical reference for U.S. petroleum data, used by traders, analysts, and Congressional Research Service.

What it can't tell you: Spot prices lag intraday futures by hours. Real-time price action sits on CME or ICE, behind paywalls. Spot is fine for trend, not for trading.

Polymarket · prediction markets

REAL-MONEY · LIVE

Polymarket runs decentralized binary contracts on real-world questions. The "market probability" is the YES-share price, denominated in USDC. Volume-weighted markets aggregate distributed information from real traders putting up real money.

What it can't tell you: Markets are biased toward U.S. liberal-leaning crypto traders. Low-volume contracts are noisy. Question framing matters enormously — "Will X happen by Date Y" is often answering a different question than the one you'd intuitively read.

GDELT · event database

NEWS-DERIVED · 15-MIN UPDATES

The Global Database of Events, Language and Tone monitors news in 65+ languages, classifies events by CAMEO type, geocodes locations, and scores tone. It surfaces what the world's press is reporting about, mapped to structured event categories.

What it can't tell you: GDELT measures media attention, not ground truth. A bombing that gets no coverage doesn't appear; a wargame that gets heavy coverage looks like a real crisis. Use it for trend, never alone.

Headlines · open press

AGGREGATED · CURATED

Pulled from GDELT's article-list mode using exact phrase matching ("iran nuclear", "iranian missile", "strait of hormuz") to filter for genuine theater coverage. Each headline links to the original outlet for verification.

What it can't tell you: No regional-language sources here (no Persian, Arabic, Hebrew). That's a real gap — most signal in this theater breaks first in regional press. Consider this a starting point, not a substitute.
